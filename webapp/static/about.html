About this Project

A Master's research project applying deep learning to structural damage detection in Albanian UNESCO World Heritage Sites — combining classification, object detection, and semantic segmentation in a single pipeline.

Motivation

The Problem

Albania's UNESCO-listed heritage sites — Berat, Gjirokastër, and Butrint — face ongoing structural deterioration from weathering, seismic activity, and neglect. Manual inspection is slow, expensive, and inconsistent. Hairline cracks that go undetected can escalate into irreversible structural failure.

The AI Solution

Deep learning enables scalable, repeatable damage assessment from photographs alone. A three-model pipeline answers three questions simultaneously: Is this structure damaged? (classification), Where are the cracks? (detection), and What fraction is damaged? (segmentation). A degradation analysis layer adds surface texture profiling and before/after change detection, enabling longitudinal monitoring across visits.

Training Data

Models were trained on four open datasets spanning 30,000+ images: OmniCrack30k (large-scale crack detection), COCO-Crack, MasonryCrack (stone & brick surfaces), and CrackForest (pavement). Fine-tuning on masonry-specific data bridges the domain gap to heritage stone walls.

Evaluation

Performance was measured on held-out test sets with standard metrics: accuracy & F1 for classification, mAP@50 for detection, and mIoU + Dice for segmentation. Cross-dataset evaluation exposed domain gaps between pavement and heritage masonry — motivating fine-tuning on masonry-specific subsets.

Degradation Analysis

Beyond single-image damage detection, the tool offers two degradation-focused analysis modes for longitudinal heritage monitoring.

Surface Texture Analysis

Analyses surface micro-structure without requiring crack labels. Produces two complementary filter-bank maps:

  • Gabor Energy — 6 orientations × 3 wavelengths. Responds strongly to crack-like elongated edges at any angle.
  • Complexity Map — local Laplacian standard deviation. Bright regions = irregular, deteriorated surface texture.
A scalar Complexity Score (0–100) summarises overall surface irregularity and flags early-stage degradation before cracks become macroscopic.

Before / After Comparison

Tracks structural change over time by comparing two photographs of the same location taken at different dates.

  • SSIM Change Map — Structural Similarity Index highlights regions where luminance, contrast, or structure changed between visits.
  • Full pipeline on both images — classification, detection, and segmentation run independently on each image, so damage extent can be compared directly.
SSIM Δ score quantifies overall structural change (0 = identical, 1 = completely different). Red hotspots on the change map pinpoint where new damage emerged.

Synthetic Degradation Stages

A synthetic temporal pipeline generates five progressive degradation stages applied to a clean masonry image — weathering, crack seeding, crack widening, spalling, severe spalling — providing ground-truth before/after pairs for validating the comparison pipeline where real multi-date photography is unavailable.

Image Preprocessing Filters

Before passing through the neural models, images can be enhanced using classical Digital Image Processing algorithms. Each filter is designed to reveal structural damage — cracks, spalling, surface deterioration — that may be difficult to detect in raw photographs. The same filter is applied consistently to both images in Before / After comparison mode.

CL

CLAHE

Histogram domain

Contrast Limited Adaptive Histogram Equalization operates on the L-channel of the LAB colour space. Unlike global equalisation, CLAHE divides the image into small tiles and redistributes each tile's histogram independently — with a clip limit to prevent noise amplification. On heritage stone, where damage often hides in shadow or low-contrast regions, CLAHE is the single most effective preprocessing step.

clipLimit = 3.0 · tileGrid = 8 × 8 · LAB L-channel only (preserves colour)
BH

Crack Extract

Morphological ops

Bilateral filtering removes high-frequency noise while preserving edge sharpness. Then the Black-Hat morphological transform — defined as close(image) − image — extracts dark thin structures against a brighter background, precisely matching the profile of cracks on weathered stone walls. The result is blended at 40% onto the denoised image, then CLAHE-enhanced.

Bilateral(d=9, σ=75) → Black-Hat(15×15 rect kernel) → CLAHE blend
FFT

FFT High-Pass

Frequency domain

The 2D Discrete Fourier Transform decomposes the L-channel into spatial frequency components. A Gaussian high-pass mask (H(u,v) = 1 − e−D²/2D₀²) attenuates low frequencies (slow-varying background illumination, large surface regions) while retaining high frequencies that correspond to crack edges and fine surface details. The high-frequency residual is blended back onto the original before CLAHE normalisation.

2D FFT → fftshift → Gaussian HP mask (D₀ = min(H,W)/8) → IFFT → blend + CLAHE
CE

Canny Overlay

Edge detection

The Canny edge detector applies bilateral pre-smoothing, then computes gradient magnitude and direction via Sobel operators. Non-maximum suppression thins edges to single-pixel width; double thresholding with hysteresis retains only strong, connected edges. Detected contours are tinted orange-red and blended at 18% opacity onto the original, highlighting crack boundaries while keeping the image recognisable to the trained neural models.

Bilateral(σ=50) → Canny(T_low=40, T_high=120) → 18% overlay blend → CLAHE
LoG

LoG Enhanced (Marr-Hildreth)

Second-order derivative

The Laplacian of Gaussian (LoG), also known as the Marr-Hildreth operator, applies a Gaussian blur (σ = 1.2) to suppress noise, then computes the Laplacian (∇²f = ∂²f/∂x² + ∂²f/∂y²) which responds maximally at intensity edges and zero-crosses at edge centres. The absolute LoG response magnitude is normalised and blended at 15% as a brightness enhancement layer, subtly boosting crack edges without replacing the image structure that the models expect.

Gaussian(σ=1.2, 5×5) → Laplacian (CV_32F) → |response| normalised → 15% blend → CLAHE

How Each Model Works

1

Classification — EfficientNet-B4

99.8% accuracy

What it does

Classifies the entire image as Cracked or Intact with a confidence score. Also produces a Grad-CAM heatmap — a gradient-weighted activation map that highlights which image regions most influenced the decision.

How it works

EfficientNet-B4 is a convolutional neural network scaled by compound coefficient (depth × width × resolution). Pretrained on ImageNet, its final layer is replaced with a 2-class head and fine-tuned on historical crack imagery at 380 × 380 px. Grad-CAM backpropagates the predicted class gradient through the last conv layer to localise discriminative regions without any spatial annotation.

Why EfficientNet-B4? Better accuracy-to-parameter ratio than ResNet50 or VGG. B4 variant hits the sweet spot for 380 px inputs — large enough for crack texture, small enough for fast inference.
2

Detection — YOLOv8s + SAHI

mAP@50 54.4%

What it does

Draws bounding boxes around individual crack regions with confidence scores. Provides spatial localisation — where in the structure damage is concentrated — which classification alone cannot give.

How it works

YOLOv8s (small variant) uses a CSPDarknet backbone with a Feature Pyramid Network for multi-scale detection. SAHI (Slicing Aided Hyper Inference) divides large images into overlapping tiles (adaptive 256–512 px), runs YOLO on each, then merges predictions with NMS. This recovers hairline cracks that would be too small to detect at full resolution. A sensitive fallback lowers the confidence threshold to 0.10 if no cracks are found at 0.25.

Why SAHI? Cracks are spatially tiny relative to the full image. Downsampling to 640 px destroys hairline detail. SAHI preserves local resolution without retraining.
3

Segmentation — U-Net / ResNet34

mIoU 83.6%

What it does

Produces a pixel-level binary mask — every pixel is classified as crack or background. Reports the percentage of the surface that is cracked, giving a quantitative damage extent beyond what bounding boxes can express.

How it works

U-Net is an encoder-decoder with skip connections. The ResNet34 encoder (ImageNet pretrained) extracts hierarchical features; the decoder upsamples them back to input resolution, with skip connections fusing fine-grained spatial detail from early encoder layers. Combined Focal + Tversky loss handles severe class imbalance (cracks occupy <5% of pixels). Input resolution is 384 × 384 px.

Why U-Net? Designed for biomedical segmentation where targets are thin and sparse — exactly the profile of crack pixels in masonry. Skip connections preserve edge sharpness that pure encoder-decoders lose.
